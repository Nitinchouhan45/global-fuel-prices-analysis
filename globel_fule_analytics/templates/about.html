1. Project Background & Core Objectives

Motor fuels (petrol, diesel, and LPG) are foundational inputs to industrial activity, logistics, personal mobility, and household cooking. While crude oil is priced in standardized global commodity markets (such as Brent Crude), retail prices paid by consumers at local pumps diverge dramatically across borders. This project investigates the structural drivers of that divergence, with a primary focus on fiscal taxation policies, national subsidy regimes, and macroeconomic income tiers.

Price Transmission Elasticity

Assess the degree and speed with which fluctuations in Brent Crude USD pass through into end-consumer petrol and diesel prices.

Taxation vs Subsidy Trade-Offs

Quantify the divergence between high-tax sovereign jurisdictions and governments deploying fiscal subsidies to shield citizens.

Regional & Income Equity

Evaluate how fuel affordability varies across World Bank income brackets (High, Upper-Middle, Lower-Middle, Low).

3. Data Preparation Methodology

Currency Standardization & Unit Normalization

Domestic pump prices originally reported in local national currencies were harmonized to USD per Liter using historical exchange rates matching the transaction dates. LPG values were converted to volumetric liter equivalents to maintain direct parity with liquid transport fuels.

Temporal Alignment & Merging

Retail survey points were aligned chronologically with daily international Brent Crude USD benchmark prices, ensuring synchronized time-series matching without look-ahead bias.

Categorical Classification

Sovereign nations were mapped to standardized continental Region groupings and World Bank Income Level tiers to enable segmented group-by aggregations and boxplot distributions.

Missing Value & Outlier Imputation

Records with non-reporting gaps in specific fuel categories were handled through verified interpolation or categorized under missing flags to prevent statistical distortion in cross-national averages.

6. Analytical Boundaries & Limitations

  • Sub-National Tax Heterogeneity: In large federal nations (e.g., United States, India, Canada), fuel taxes vary substantially by state or province. National aggregations reflect nationwide averages and may smooth over regional extremes.
  • Currency Fluctuation Distortions: Converting retail prices to USD introduces foreign exchange rate volatility. A weakening domestic currency may make pump prices appear cheaper in USD even as domestic consumers face sharp inflation.
  • Indirect Subsidies: While explicit price caps and subsidies are recorded, implicit non-cash subsidies (e.g., state-owned oil enterprise debt absorption) are difficult to isolate in public datasets.
  • Survey Latency: Fuel price reporting frequency varies across jurisdictions, meaning some developing nation datapoints may reflect monthly surveys rather than high-frequency spot adjustments.
